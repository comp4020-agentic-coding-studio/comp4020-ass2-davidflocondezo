# Canon & Scripture

**What makes a text sacred — and what doesn't.**

---

## Canon was never about truth

It was about a community deciding to keep referring back to a text.

That's a testable process, not a vibe.

---

{/* _class: banner */}

## The screenshot-survives-context test

A canonical post still means something when it's:

- screenshotted out of its original thread
- cited by people who never saw the original
- read months after the moment that produced it

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "Scripture isn't the text that's true. It's the text that's still being cited."

---

## Our primary sources

Reddit and X aren't scripture despite being unreliable.

They're scripture **because** so much of what actually gets re-cited already
lives there, tested by exactly this process.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Treating them as primary sources

isn't a joke at scripture's expense.

It's applying the exact same test scripture has always had to pass.

---

## This week, in the session

- bring a post you think has already passed the test
- argue for its canonicity against the group's objections
- learn to tell "popular" from "canonical" — they're not the same claim
