A prophecy is a claim about a crisis or a reveal that hasn't happened yet, and
what separates a false prophet from a true one was never how confidently the
claim was delivered — both deliver with total confidence. It's whether the
claim survives contact with the event it predicted. Misinformation is best
read as prophecy of this kind: urgent, specific, and stated as though the
speaker already has privileged access to what's coming.

False prophecy travels faster for a structural reason, not a moral one: a true
prophecy is constrained by what will actually happen, while a false one is
only constrained by what sounds urgent enough to share immediately. Urgency,
not evidence, is the variable that predicts spread, and false prophets are
free to maximise it without limit.

## Outline

- prophecy as a claim about a coming event, judged only after the fact
- misinformation as prophecy delivered with borrowed urgency
- why false prophecy spreads faster: unconstrained by the outcome, true isn't
