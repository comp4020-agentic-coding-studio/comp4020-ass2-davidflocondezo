A sermon and a rage-bait post are built from the same three parts. An opening
line claims attention before the audience knows what's being argued. A
provocation follows, engineered to produce a felt reaction — outrage,
vindication, disbelief — ahead of any reasoned one. And a close reveals the
whole structure was never trying to prove anything; it was trying to produce
exactly that reaction, and the reaction is the point.

Homiletics, not apologetics, is the right frame for this because homiletics
has never claimed to be proof. It's persuasion toward a response, judged by
whether the response happens, not by whether the claim survives scrutiny
afterward — which is precisely how rage-bait is judged too, by its replies and
its shares, never by whether it was right.

## Outline

- the shared three-part structure: hook, provocation, bait-and-switch
- homiletics over apologetics: persuasion toward reaction, not proof
- how a rage-bait post is judged, and why it's never "was this true"
