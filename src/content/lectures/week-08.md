A rage-bait post works the same three-part structure a persuasive speech
does — hook, provocation, close — but that's the last thing this course
needs another paragraph of metaphor for. What matters is *why* it works:
outrage-inducing content gets shared roughly twice as often as neutral
content on the same topic, because sharing something that makes you angry
functions as a public performance of your own values, not a private
reaction. Classical rhetoric already had a name for this: pathos-first
argument, where the emotional response is secured before any claim is
actually evaluated, and where ethos does its work not through evidence but
through the confident register of the delivery itself — certainty reads as
credibility whether or not it's earned.

The engagement mechanics reward this deliberately. A comment written in
anger is longer, faster, and more likely to be replied to than a considered
one, and each reply is itself a fresh engagement signal that pushes the
post further — which is why a rage-bait post's real audience is never the
people it convinces, only the people it provokes into replying.

## How to rage-bait

1. **Pick a claim with a live fault line** — a disagreement people already
   hold an identity-linked opinion on, not a fact they're neutral about.
   There's no outrage to harvest from a claim nobody's staked anything on.
2. **Open with the provocation, not the setup.** State the position flatly
   in the first line, before any qualification — qualifying first gives the
   reader permission to read calmly, which is the one thing you can't
   afford.
3. **Withhold the reasoning.** Give a conclusion, not an argument for it; an
   actual argument invites engagement with the *claim*, a bare assertion
   invites engagement with the *poster*.
4. **Bait the reply, not the agreement.** Leave one deliberately contestable
   detail — a number, a comparison — so disagreement has something concrete
   to grab onto and reply to, rather than a vague feeling with nowhere to
   land.
5. **Never resolve it.** A close that concedes a point kills the thread; the
   post's job ends the moment the reaction starts, not when the argument
   does.

## Outline

- why outrage-inducing content outperforms neutral content: sharing as public performance
- pathos-first argument and confidence-as-credibility, not proof
- how the reply mechanics compound: anger produces engagement that produces more reach
- how to rage-bait: five steps, and why each one targets the mechanism, not the metaphor
