Canon-formation was never about a text being true; it was about a community
deciding to keep referring back to it. That's a testable process, not a
vibe: a canonical post survives being screenshotted out of its original
context, gets cited by people who never saw the original thread, and keeps
meaning something months after the moment that produced it has passed.

A screenshot strips out every signal that let the original audience judge a
post correctly — the poster's history, the reply chain it was answering,
the norms of the specific space it was posted in. What travels afterward is
the bare claim, with the context that would let a new reader weigh it
removed. That's why a joke posted for one community reads as a sincere
statement in another, and why an out-of-context screenshot is the single
most effective vector for turning something true-in-context into something
false-in-general — this is called context collapse, and it's a real,
well-documented failure mode of any platform where a post can be extracted
from the feed it was written for.

Source evaluation for anything spreading virally has a specific procedure
underneath it. Find the original post, not a repost of it. Check whether the
account has a coherent history in the same voice — a burner or compromised
account posting one uncharacteristic message is the most common tell.
Look for independent convergence rather than volume: ten reposts of the
same screenshot is one data point, while the same claim appearing
separately, in different words, from accounts with no connection to each
other, is real corroboration.

## How to verify before you cite

1. **Find the source, not the screenshot.** An image with no link back to
   the original post has already lost the one piece of context that would
   let you check it.
2. **Check the account's history, not just the one post.** A single
   sincere-sounding post from an account with no other activity in that
   voice is the clearest sign of a compromised or bad-faith account.
3. **Look for independent convergence, not volume.** Ten reposts of the same
   screenshot is one data point; the same claim appearing separately, in
   different words, from unconnected accounts, is real corroboration.
4. **Check the date.** Old images and quotes get recirculated as current
   events constantly; a reverse-image or exact-phrase search usually
   surfaces the original moment.
5. **Assume context collapse by default.** If a post reads as extreme or
   surprising, the likelier explanation is that something got stripped out,
   not that you've found something no one else noticed.
