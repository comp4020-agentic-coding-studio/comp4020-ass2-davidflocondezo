Canon-formation was never about a text being true; it was about a community
deciding to keep referring back to it. That's a testable process, not a vibe:
a canonical post survives being screenshotted out of its original context,
gets cited by people who never saw the original thread, and keeps meaning
something months after the moment that produced it has passed.

Reddit and X function as this course's scripture for exactly that reason —
not because either platform is authoritative, but because their posts are
where a huge fraction of actually-canonical text currently lives, tested and
re-tested by exactly this kind of communal re-citation. Treating them as
primary sources isn't a joke at scripture's expense; it's applying the same
test scripture has always had to pass.
