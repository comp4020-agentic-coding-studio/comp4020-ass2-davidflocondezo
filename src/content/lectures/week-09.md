No saint declares themselves one. Sainthood is conferred retroactively, by a
community that keeps venerating someone after the fact — which is exactly how
virality works, and exactly why neither can be manufactured by the person it
happens to. What a creator or a post can do is become venerable, and the thing
that makes veneration repeatable, generation after generation of new viewers,
is the icon: one compressed, instantly recognisable image, gesture, or sound
that stands in for the whole canonised body of work.

That's what canonization actually tests for, and it's what this week's
assessment asks students to argue for directly: not "is this popular," but
"has this earned the specific, durable, instantly-recognisable status that
lets an audience keep recognising and re-venerating it."

## Outline

- sainthood as conferred after the fact, never self-declared
- the icon: the compressed symbol that makes veneration repeatable
- canonization as the real test behind "did this go viral"
