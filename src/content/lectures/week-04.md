Apocryphal and pseudepigraphal texts were never rejected for being poorly
written — some are extraordinary. They were rejected for lacking
provenance: attributed to an author who didn't write them, claiming a
lineage they don't have. That's the exact failure mode of AI slop. It
borrows every surface feature of sincere, well-sourced writing while being
traceable to no author, no lived experience, no event.

The features that make generated text read as sincere are specific and
learnable, not mysterious. A consistent register held across an unnaturally
long stretch is one: a real person's writing drifts in tense, formality, and
in-jokes across a long post, where generated text tends to hold one register
throughout. Rounded, even-handed moral framing is another — real strong
opinions carry some careless, unpolished edge that generated text tends to
smooth off. And plausible-but-uncheckable detail is a third: a named but
untraceable place, a specific-sounding but unverifiable statistic, a person
described precisely enough to sound real but never findable by search.

What none of this can fake is provenance itself. A real post is anchored to
something outside the text — a timestamp that matches an event, an account
with a consistent history, a detail an independent search can confirm.
Slop can imitate every feature of the text and still fail this one test,
because provenance isn't a textual feature at all; it's a fact about the
world that the text can't manufacture from inside itself. That's what
canon-formation was always testing for too, which is why this week follows
directly from the last.

## How to create a believable AI-generated post

1. **Anchor one detail in something checkable**, even a small one — a real
   place, date, or number — so a skim-verification passes even though the
   piece as a whole has no provenance behind it.
2. **Let the register drift slightly.** Perfectly consistent tone across a
   long post is itself a tell; real writing wanders in formality and gets a
   little rougher toward the end.
3. **Include one unpolished, slightly unfair opinion.** Sincere writing
   usually contains a claim the writer hasn't fully thought through;
   smoothing every edge off is what makes generated text feel hollow.
4. **Use unverifiable-but-precise detail deliberately, not by accident** — a
   name or number that sounds specific but can't be searched is exactly the
   failure mode this week studies, so plant it on purpose and be ready to
   name it.
5. **Know your own missing provenance signal before anyone else finds it.**
   A real post could always answer "who are you and how do you know this";
   yours should have a specific, nameable gap there — that gap is what
   you're graded on showing.
