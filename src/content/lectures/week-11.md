Speaking in tongues was never meant to communicate information to an outside
listener — its function was to mark, audibly, that the speaker belonged to a
community sharing an experience outsiders couldn't access. Slang that a
cohort's parents can't parse does the identical work: the opacity isn't a
side-effect of the language changing too fast to keep up with, it's the
feature that makes the language worth having.

That's why fluency here isn't measured by vocabulary alone. Anyone can learn a
list of terms; the actual test is whether the terms still carry meaning when
translated out to plain English and back, the way any living language survives
translation, rather than collapsing into a glossary of things that used to
mean something.

## Outline

- glossolalia as belonging marked by opacity, not information transferred
- Gen Alpha slang's opacity to outsiders as the feature, not the bug
- fluency tested by round-trip translation, not vocabulary alone
