Speaking in tongues was never meant to communicate information to an
outside listener — its function was to mark, audibly, that the speaker
belonged to a community sharing an experience outsiders couldn't access.
Slang that a cohort's parents can't parse does the identical work.

In-group language does real communicative work beyond marking belonging.
It compresses long context into a single word — a specific piece of slang
can carry an entire shared reference in one syllable that would otherwise
take a paragraph to explain to an outsider. Its opacity to outsiders is
also what economists would call a costly signal: using it correctly is hard
to fake without actually having spent time in the community, which is
exactly why it functions as proof of belonging rather than just a
stylistic choice.

Slang terms drift in meaning quickly and in a predictable direction. A term
usually starts narrow and sincere, widens as it's picked up outside its
origin, and often flattens or flips in meaning once it's adopted by the
audience it was originally used to describe from outside — a pattern
linguists call semantic bleaching. Fluent speakers code-switch constantly
and unconsciously, adjusting register between a group chat and a caption
meant for a wider audience, and it's that unconscious switching — not
vocabulary size — that a real fluency test measures.

## How to tell fluent from performed

1. **Test for code-switching, not vocabulary.** A fluent speaker adjusts
   register automatically depending on audience; someone who learned a
   word-list uses the same register everywhere, including where it doesn't
   fit.
2. **Check for drift-awareness.** Fluent use tracks a term's current
   meaning, including when it's shifted or flattened from its origin; a
   lagging use of an older sense is the clearest outsider tell.
3. **Try the round trip.** Translate the term to plain English and back with
   someone else who's fluent — if the meaning survives the round trip
   intact, it's a real semantic unit, not a keyword.
4. **Watch who uses it in front of whom.** A costly signal is one withheld
   from outsiders on purpose; slang used identically regardless of audience
   has usually already lost its in-group function.
5. **Expect it to keep moving.** Any slang stable enough to be fully learned
   from the outside has probably already widened past the point of
   signalling much at all.
