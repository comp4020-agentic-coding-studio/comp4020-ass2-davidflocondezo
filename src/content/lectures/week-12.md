Every doctrine so far has trained something you do alone, in your own feed, on
your own time. Missiology turns the semester outward: the Great Commission
isn't "believe this," it's "go and make others believe it" — recruit a friend
into your feed, your creators, your slang, and treat their conversion as the
actual outcome, not a side-effect of your own practice.

Missiology is also traditionally the discipline where a tradition, having
finished defending its own canon, starts proposing new doctrine for questions
the existing canon didn't cover. The capstone asks exactly that: having spent
eleven weeks learning this course's method — how canon forms, what counts as
apocryphal, what makes something venerable — apply that method yourself, to a
corner of brain rot this course never got to, and argue for its place
alongside the rest.

## Outline

- the Great Commission: recruitment as the outcome, not a side-effect
- missiology as the discipline that proposes new doctrine, not just defends old
- the capstone: apply the semester's method to build a doctrine of your own
