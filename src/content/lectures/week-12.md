Every doctrine so far has trained something you do alone, in your own feed,
on your own time. Missiology turns the semester outward: the Great
Commission isn't "believe this," it's "go and make others believe it."

Communities that grow by broadcasting content alone grow linearly — one
creator, reaching a bounded audience, no matter how good the content gets.
Communities that grow through recruitment grow differently, because each
new member becomes a potential source of further members, not just another
consumer — the same compounding structure product-growth teams call a
referral loop, and it's why a community with a mediocre core offering but a
strong recruitment mechanic will often outgrow a much better one with no
such mechanic at all.

The value of joining also rises with the number of people already in it — a
slang, a format, or an in-joke is worth more to a new member the more
people already share it, which is a real network effect, not just a vague
sense of popularity. That's why the missionary's actual job was never
"explain why this is good," it's "lower the cost of the first step" — one
specific piece of shared vocabulary, one creator to follow first, a single
clear on-ramp rather than the whole culture at once — because the harder
the first step, the more of the compounding effect never gets a chance to
start.

## How to recruit someone

1. **Give them one on-ramp, not the whole culture.** A single creator, term,
   or format to start with converts far better than "you just have to see
   it all."
2. **Recruit into a specific role, not a general audience.** Someone who
   joins to do something — reply, remix, contribute — is more likely to
   recruit the next person than someone who joins only to watch.
3. **Make the first success fast.** A referral loop compounds only if the
   new member gets an early, real payoff — a joke landing, a post getting
   noticed — within their first attempts, not after a long ramp-up.
4. **Lower the cost of explaining it.** The easier a piece of the culture is
   to hand to someone else in one sentence, the faster it propagates person
   to person.
5. **Track who's recruiting, not just who's here.** A community sustained by
   broadcast growth alone plateaus at its creator's reach; one where members
   recruit members is the only version that compounds.
