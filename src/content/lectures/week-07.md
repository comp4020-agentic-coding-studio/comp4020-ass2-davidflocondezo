A liturgical calendar disciplines devotion by making it recur on a fixed,
knowable schedule. The trend calendar disciplines the same way without ever
announcing the schedule in advance — a sound, a format, or a phrase becomes
the week's feast day with no warning, and stops being one just as suddenly.

Recency bias is built into most ranking systems on purpose: a system that
scores how fast something is accumulating engagement right now, rather than
its total engagement to date, is what lets a new format displace an older,
more-liked one within hours. That design choice is exactly why trend
velocity feels structural rather than arbitrary — the system is explicitly
scoring for "rising," not "good," and a post rising slowly loses to one
rising fast even with a smaller total. A trend's lifecycle follows a
recognisable shape on top of that: a discovery phase where a small number of
accounts use a format sincerely, a growth phase where it's picked up faster
than it can be explained, a peak where its mechanics are common knowledge,
and a decline phase — usually within days to a couple of weeks — where using
it unironically reads as behind rather than current.

Posting into the discovery or growth phase reads as fluent, regardless of
polish; posting into the decline phase reads as finding out last, regardless
of how well-made the post is. Timing outranks craft here in a way it
doesn't in most other doctrines this course teaches.

## How to catch a trend before it peaks

1. **Watch for format, not content.** A trend is usually a reusable
   structure — a sound, a caption pattern, an edit style — applied to
   different content, so track structures rather than individual viral
   posts.
2. **Post in the growth phase, not once you've seen it everywhere.** By the
   time a format is unavoidable in your own feed, it's closer to its peak
   than its start — your feed is a lagging indicator, not a leading one.
3. **Weight rising engagement over absolute engagement.** A six-hour-old
   post with fast growth is a better signal of a live window than a
   week-old post with a higher total.
4. **Move fast once you commit.** A trend rewards presence over polish, so
   ship something rough inside the live window rather than a well-made post
   after it's closed.
5. **Recognise the decline phase and stop.** Using a format past its peak
   doesn't read as timeless — it reads as late, and the same recency bias
   that made you visible on the way up makes a late entry nearly invisible.
