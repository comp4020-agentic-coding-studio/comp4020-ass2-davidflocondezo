> Forge a text that reads as scripture. Then say, precisely, what makes it a
> forgery.

Convincing AI slop fails the same way apocryphal texts always did: not on
craft, but on provenance — no traceable author, no verifiable event, no
lineage of re-citation to check it against. This assessment asks you to
produce that failure on purpose, in a real content format, and then to
diagnose exactly where the provenance breaks down.

## Part A — the composition

Write a piece of AI-generated-style slop (500–800 words) that imitates a
canonical form covered in weeks 1–3: a devotional post, a thread with the
rhythm of received wisdom, a caption with the moral clarity of scripture. It
should read as sincere on a first pass.

## Part B — the breakdown

In 300–500 words, identify the specific formal features your composition
borrows from real canon (tone, structure, rhetorical moves) and the specific
markers of provenance it lacks (no traceable author, no verifiable event, no
lineage of re-citation). This part is marked on precision, not on agreeing
that slop is bad.

## What you submit

A single document containing both parts, clearly labelled Part A and Part B.
