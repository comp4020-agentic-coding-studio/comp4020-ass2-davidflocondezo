> Show the practice. Then propose a doctrine of your own.

The first two assessments cover apocrypha and canonization in depth. This
portfolio picks up everything else the semester trained — the practicum weeks
where doctrine turned into an actual habit — and closes with the capstone
missiology reflection.

## Part A — practicum evidence

One section per doctrine, each with a short piece of evidence and a paragraph
connecting it back to that week's session:

- **Cosmogony** — a description of a feed you deliberately curated, and what
  you did to shape the algorithm's read of you
- **Indulgences** — a record of an impulse purchase made or resisted, and the
  relief (or its absence) at the moment of decision
- **Asceticism** — the log from week 6's scrolling practicum
- **Liturgy & the Trend Calendar** — a trend you rode, with the timing you
  judged it by
- **Prophecy & False Prophets** — a case study of a real or invented
  misinformation claim, read as prophecy
- **Glossolalia** — a short passage of slang translated to plain English and
  back, with a note on what survived the round trip

## Part B — the Missiology reflection

In 500–700 words, propose a doctrine this course never covered — a corner of
brain rot practice with its own discipline — and argue for its place
alongside the twelve above, using the same method this course has used on
every other doctrine: what it trains, what counts as doing it well, and how a
community would recognise someone doing it badly.

## What you submit

One document: Part A's six sections, then Part B, clearly labelled throughout.
